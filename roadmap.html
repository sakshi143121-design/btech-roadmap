Your 4-year roadmap

A clear plan for each year: what to learn, how to practice, what to build, and how to know you are ready to move on. Tap a year to open it.

How to use this roadmap: it is a guide, not a strict rule. Go at your own speed, and borrow things from another year if you feel ready. The most important habit is small, regular practice, not studying for 10 hours once a month.

Year 1: Learn the basics
  • Goal: build a strong base and the habit of coding every day.
  • Semester 1: pick one language (C, C++ or Python, usually the one your college teaches). Learn input and output, variables, if-else and loops.
  • Semester 2: learn functions, arrays and strings, and solve small problems. Start HTML and CSS.
  • Why these: loops and conditions are the base of every program. HTML and CSS let you see your work on screen, so learning feels fun.
  • Weekly routine: 1 hour of coding on 5 days, one day for a small project, one day to rest.
  • Projects, easy to harder: calculator, number guessing game, to-do list, personal page.
  • Internship: not needed yet. Join a coding club, or attend a hackathon to watch and learn.
  • You are ready for Year 2 if you can: write a program with loops and functions without copying, solve easy problems alone, and make a simple web page.
  • Common mistake: jumping between many languages and tutorials. Finish one course first.

Year 2: Build your core
  • Goal: get strong at problem solving and build your first real projects.
  • Semester 3: DSA basics: arrays, strings, recursion, linked lists, stacks and queues. Learn OOP in your language.
  • Semester 4: trees, sorting and searching, SQL basics, JavaScript, and Git with GitHub.
  • Why these: companies test DSA to see how you think. Git is how teams share and save code.
  • Weekly routine: 3 to 4 DSA problems, one project session, and one day to revise your notes.
  • Projects, easy to harder: tic tac toe, quiz website, student management app with a database, and a portfolio site live on GitHub Pages.
  • Internship: you can start now. A short first internship in DSA, web development or Python is a good start. Check that the company is real, and be careful with ones that ask for money.
  • You are ready for Year 3 if you: solve easy problems and some medium ones, use Git basics, and have 2 projects on GitHub.
  • Common mistake: watching videos without practicing. Always code along.

Year 3: Go deeper
  • Goal: choose a path and prove your skills with projects and an internship.
  • Semester 5: choose your path. Keep practicing DSA with medium problems, and study OS, DBMS and networks with your college subjects.
  • Semester 6: build one big project in your path, prepare your resume, and apply for a summer internship.
  • Paths to choose from: web development (React, Node, databases), app development, AI and ML (Python and maths), cybersecurity, or cloud basics.
  • Weekly routine: 4 to 5 DSA problems, and 6 to 8 hours on your chosen path.
  • Projects: a full website with login and a database, a project that solves a real problem for your college, and a small fix on an open source project.
  • Internship: aim for 2 to 3 months in the summer. Get a one-page resume, GitHub and LinkedIn ready first, and apply early.
  • You are ready for Year 4 if you have: one deep project online, one internship or a strong project, and a finished resume.
  • Common mistake: learning a little of everything. Go deep in one path.

Year 4: Get job-ready
  • Goal: be ready for placements or your next step.
  • Semester 7: revise DSA, OS, DBMS, networks and OOP. Practice aptitude and reasoning, and start mock interviews.
  • Semester 8: finish your final-year project, do an internship if you can, and keep applying.
  • Weekly routine: one mock interview, DSA practice on most days, and aptitude practice on 3 days.
  • Projects: a final-year project with a clear README, a live link and a short demo video.
  • Next steps: placements, GATE or higher studies, a startup, or government exams. Keep one backup option.
  • You are ready if you can: explain each project in 2 minutes, solve medium problems in time, and show a strong resume.
  • Common mistake: starting preparation only when placements begin. Start in Year 3.

4 habits for all 4 years

  • Code a little every day. Small and regular beats big and rare.
  • Put your work on GitHub. It is your proof that you can build.
  • Ask for help early. Seniors, teachers and online communities save you weeks.
  • Keep short notes. Write what you learned in your own words.
