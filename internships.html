Internships, made simple

What an internship is, when to start, how to prepare, where to apply, and how to stay safe from fake offers.

What is an internship?

An internship is a short period, usually 1 to 3 months, where you work with a company or team and learn on real tasks. It shows you what the job is really like, and it makes your resume much stronger.

When to start? You can start in Year 2 with a short, simple internship. Year 3 summer is the best time for a longer one. Do not wait for the final year.

Get ready first

Before you apply, check these 5 things:

  • A one-page resume with your projects and skills
  • A GitHub profile with at least 2 projects
  • A LinkedIn profile with a clear photo and short intro
  • Basic skills in one area, like web development, Python or DSA
  • The ability to explain each project in 2 minutes

Where to find internships

  • Internship websites: like Internshala and LinkedIn Jobs.
  • Company career pages: many companies post internships on their own website.
  • Your college placement cell: ask them about companies that visit your college.
  • Seniors and alumni: a message to a senior can lead to a referral.
  • Open source and hackathons: they build experience and connections.

How to apply

Send a short, polite message with your resume and GitHub link. You can copy this and change the words in brackets:

Hello [Name], I am [Your Name], a B.Tech CSE student in Year [X] at [College]. I have built [Project 1] and [Project 2] (GitHub: [link]). I am looking for an internship in [web development / Python / DSA] and would love to learn from your team. My resume is attached. Thank you for your time.

Apply to many places, not just one. Do not feel sad about no reply. Many companies reply late, or never. Keep applying and keep improving.

What will they ask in the interview?
  • "Tell me about yourself", so prepare a 1-minute answer
  • Your projects: what you built, why, and what was hard
  • Basic questions on your language and DSA
  • "Why do you want this internship?"
Should I pay for an internship?

Be very careful. A real internship gives you work and learning. Some are paid, and many are unpaid, but you should not be asked to pay money to get the offer. Fake offers that ask for a fee are common. If someone asks for money, think twice, and talk to a senior or teacher first.

How do I check if an internship is real?
  • Search the company's name, website and LinkedIn page
  • Check that the email address uses the company's own domain
  • Ask for the work details and an official offer letter
  • Ask seniors if they know the company
Is a certificate enough?

No. A certificate helps, but companies care more about what you built and what you learned. Keep a small record of your tasks and projects, and add them to your resume and GitHub.

How do I get the most out of an internship?
  • Ask questions when you are stuck, but try first
  • Write a short note every week about what you learned
  • Finish your tasks on time and ask for feedback
  • At the end, ask for a certificate or a recommendation
  • Add the work to your resume and post about it on LinkedIn
